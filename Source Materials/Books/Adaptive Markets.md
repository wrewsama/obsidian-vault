## chapter 1
- Efficient Market Hypothesis: Prices fully reflect all available information
- the price of an event on prediction markets reflects (the market's assessment of) its likelihood

## chapter 2
- humans aren't _homo economicus_ who make only perfectly rational decisions
    - e.g. losing hurts more than winning feels good
- risk: randomness that can be measured or quantified
- uncertainty: randomness that can't, e.g. using a completely new, unproven technology

## chapter 3
- the study of psychology  / neuroscience can help to explain the irrational behaviour of humans in the market 

## chapter 4
- emotion is an integral part of human decision-making, we would make terrible decisions without emotions like fear
- humans aren't a rational animal, we're a _rationalising_ animal that interprets the world around us by forming a narrative, that we then use to predict the future and make present decisions
- intelligence is just the ability to generate accurate cause-and-effect descriptions of reality (aka a creating good narrative)

## chapter 5
- evolution: change in characteristics passed between generations 
- mutation + selection
- humans can now evolve at the speed of thought as we can iterate on decisions instead of waiting generations for mutations

## chapter 6
- humans don't actually optimise, we _satisfice_ (satisfy + suffice) based on heuristics
- those heuristics evolve at the speed of thought as we modify them based on our experiences (positive and negative feedback)
- consumer behaviour reflects their past evolutionary economic environment

## chapter 7
- hedging: long one instrument, short another => limits losses when the market as a whole goes down
- hedge funds also follow the same phenomenon as evolution
