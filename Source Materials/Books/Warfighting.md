## The Nature of War
- Definition: struggle between 2 hostile wills, each trying to impose itself on the other
    - continuous mutual adaptation, like 2 wrestlers applying force and counterforce
- key elements
    - friction: reduce and prepare to deal with obstacles
    - uncertainty: be ready to respond to unknown variables
    - fluidity: be ready to adapt to changing variables
    - disorder: things will go wrong, prepare for it
    - complexity: war is dictated by the collective behaviour of countless parts
    - humans: **human will, instilled through leadership, is the driving force of all action in war**
    - violence and danger: not the absence of fear, but the strength to overcome it
    - physical, moral, and mental forces: do not neglect moral and mental factors
    - evolution: understand which aspects of war are likely to change and which are not
    - the science, art, and dynamic of war: respectively, hard knowledge (e.g. ballistics and mechanics), creative skills (e.g. strategy), and social skills (e.g. fortitude, esprit)

## The Theory of War
- War serves one's political goals
- levels of war (top to bottom): strategic / operational / tactical
- all actions in war are based on either initiative or response. Prefer initiative.
- 2 styles of war: attrition (destruction via superior firepower) and manuever (circumventing problems and attacking it from advantageous positions)
- keys to combat power: exploiting opportunity through
    - speed + focus
    - surprise + boldness
- enemy's centre of gravity: the factors that are essential for the enemy's success
- enemy's critical vulnerabilities: areas of significance that the enemy does not expect to be attacked

## Preparing for War
- during times of peace, the most important task of any military is to prepare for war
- force planning: identify required capabilities and implementing programs to develop those capabilities
- organisation
- doctrine: fundamental, common beliefs
- professionalism: expertise, both in action and intellect
- training: both drills (standardised, repeatable techniques) and exercises (free-form and uncertain)
- professional military education: for creative, thinking leaders
- personnel management: stability and advancement
- equipment: 
    - should be easy to operate and maintain, reliable, and interoperable with other equipment
    - increase in combat effectiveness must outweight the cost of supporting that equipment

## The Conduct of War
- key traits to achieve maneuver warfare
    - endurance
    - courage
    - flexibility of mind
    - self-discipline
- orient on the enemy: understand their system
- shaping the action: conceive how we want to win
    - project thoughts through time and space
    - the further away, the less precision we should attempt to impose
- 2 parts to every mission: the _task_ to be performed and the _intent_ behind it
- use combined arms to create dilemmas for the enemy (e.g. SAW + M203)
