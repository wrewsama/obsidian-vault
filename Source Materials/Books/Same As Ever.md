- Risk is what you don't see coming. Invest in preparedness, not prediction
- people with abnormal success need to be abnormal too
- stories are more powerful than statistics
- Carl Jung's idea of **enantiodromia**: an excess of something leads to its opposite
- stress is where the magic happens
    - most technological innovations started for military purposes (e.g. nuclear power, airplanes, cars)
    - the Great Depression was also the period of greatest productivity
- most things are complex to make, yet simple to break
- plan like a pessimist and dream like an optimist
- trying to min-max everything to achieve perfection often leaves you even worse off than a "good" approach

> "Every job comes with pieces you don't like. And we need to say: That's part of it." - Jeff Bezos
- It's supposed to hurt, endure it and don't obsess over some quick hack or shortcut
- All competitive advantages disappear in time. Keep running.
- You don't truly understand things until you've experienced them firsthand. Both the good and the bad.
> "The sore truth is that complexity sells better"  - Edgar Djikstra
- humans find complexity more attractive, even though simplicity works better
- when you encounter someone with a view you can't understand, ask "what have you experienced that I haven't that makes you believe what you do?"
- instead of trying to predict what's changing in the future, look at the broad patterns across history that never changed
