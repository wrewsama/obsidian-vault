- problems
    - kind problems: fixed, repeating patterns - best done by specialists
    - wicked problems: always changing - generalists have the advantage
- example of general reasoning skill: Fermi problems
- use interleaved practice to find deep structural similarities between different tasks
- use analogical thinking, the more unrelated the topic, the better
- don't be afraid to quit a field and start a new one

> "You have to carry a big basket to bring something home." --Fraces Hesselbein
- open your mind to learn something from any diverse experience
- sticking to a specialisation is okay, but we need to be open minded to try enough things to know what to specialise in

- explore lateral thinking with withered technology: finding new uses for old things
- learning sometimes requires putting aside your experiences / views
- don't let others make you feel like you're behind
- maintain the mindset of experimentation: most things won't be useful, but some will be life-changing
- note that there's nothing inherently wrong with specialisation either
